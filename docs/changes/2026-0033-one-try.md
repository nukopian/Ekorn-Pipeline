# 2026-0033. One try for each pulled value

**Status:** open

**Decisions:** D1 settled: an idea for after v0.3.0. None open.

**Origin:** 2026-0025's D7, from 2026-0032's What's left; tried on
2026-11-14

## Background

Each value pulled through stages enters two `try` blocks: one around
the source's step, one around the stages. 2026-0032 put each at about
0.07 µs, and one for both at about 6% on `next`.

## Decisions

- [x] **D1.** Kept, dropped, or an idea? **An idea for after v0.3.0,**
  Iain decided on 2026-11-14, once it was tried and measured, as below.

## What happened

**Tried, 2026-11-14,** on `develop` at
eed4ecabfe3459c1ff898036030a881e447be265, and set aside. With stages,
the step ran in the stages' `try`, a `$pulling` of 1 while it ran
telling the `catch` the source died, and 2 once it gave a value; with
none, it kept a `try` of its own, the source's error handling shared
by a closure. Every test passed, and a script comparing what a step,
a stage and an end see of `ending` and `run_context`, through deaths,
stops and nested runs, printed the same as before.

To see the same, a value from the source must have its `local`s set
inside the `try`, after the step, as the step sees its caller's; and
the stages' `catch` sets `ending` again, as the ends it calls saw it
set.

Best of five, interleaved, in seconds for 300,000 values:

| Case | Before | One `try` | Gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| `all`, one stage | 0.324 | 0.320 | 1% |
| `all`, three stages | 0.460 | 0.457 | 1% |
| a list sink last | 0.500 | 0.497 | 1% |
| `next`, one stage | 0.304 | 0.298 | 2% |
| `next`, no stages | 0.127 | 0.135 | −6% |
| pushed, one stage (unchanged) | 0.178 | 0.179 | none |

Found: entering a `try` costs less than 0.07 µs on 5.36. In a
benchmark of the `try` alone, one containing a `next`, even one not
taken, ran about 20% slower than one without, and one containing a
`local` about 9%, so the shared `try` had no `next` in it. With no
stages, a shared
`try` was 52% slower, as a value from the source went through the
stages' set-up, where it had returned at once; returning from inside
the `try` cut that to 12%, and only a `try` of its own matched the old
loop. The `local`s inside the `try` cost about 2% of `next` with one
stage, most of what was gained.

What might yet pay: a loop that sets `ending` and `run_context` once
for a stretch of values, not once a value, as 2026-0028's plain loop
has one `try` for a stretch.
